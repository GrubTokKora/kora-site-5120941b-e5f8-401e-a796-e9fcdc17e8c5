The treatment

What it does for your skin

Microdermabrasion is a non-invasive exfoliation and rejuvenation treatment. I use fine crystals to remove dead surface cells, which smooths texture, brightens dullness, and reduces the look of fine lines and sun damage.

I like it for clients who want brighter-looking skin with minimal downtime. Most people see their skin look smoother and brighter after one session. For sun spots, texture and pores, the best results build with consistency.

What it targets

  • 01Rough or uneven texture
  • 02Dullness
  • 03The look of fine lines
  • 04The look of sun damage and superficial sun spots

On melanated skin

I work carefully, especially with melanated skin, because the goal is glow without triggering irritation that can leave dark marks behind. I pay close attention to how your skin responds, and if I think a patch test is the smarter move, we’ll do that first.

When I’ll suggest something else

If your pigment is deeper or stubborn, I’ll tell you plainly and talk you through options like a CHEMICAL PEEL or another resurfacing approach, so you’re not spinning your wheels.

Questions

Before your microdermabrasion

Is microdermabrasion good for sun spots and uneven skin tone?

Yes, it can be a great choice for sun spots and uneven tone when the discoloration is more superficial. I like it for clients who want brighter-looking skin with minimal downtime. If the pigment is deeper or stubborn, I’ll talk you through options like a chemical peel or another resurfacing approach so you’re not spinning your wheels.

How many sessions will I need to see results?

Most people see their skin look smoother and brighter after a session, but the best results for sun spots, texture and pores usually build with consistency. A common plan is 3 to 6 sessions spaced 2 to 4 weeks apart, then maintenance as needed depending on what you’re targeting.

Does it hurt, and how much downtime is there?

It’s usually very tolerable. You’ll feel a strong exfoliation and suction, but it shouldn’t feel painful. Downtime is minimal for most people: expect mild redness for about 24 to 48 hours, and you can typically return to work the same day.

What should I avoid before and after, including sun?

Afterwards, avoid heavy sun exposure for at least 2 weeks and wear SPF daily. Skip harsh scrubs and aggressive actives for a few days so your skin can settle. Tell me what you’re currently using and I’ll help you adjust your routine, so you protect your results instead of irritating your skin.

Can microdermabrasion be used safely on darker skin tones?

Often, yes. The key is a careful, skin-first approach that reduces the risk of irritation leading to post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation. I pay close attention to how your skin responds, and if I think a patch test is the smarter move, we’ll do that.
